## What HengHost actually is

HengHost is the IDC brand under Hong Kong-based SonderCloud Limited, founded in 2010. That puts it at roughly 16 years of operation — long enough that "new kid on the block" accusations don't really stick. The company is a certified member of both APNIC and ARIN, holds its own ASN, and runs a meaningful pool of IPv4/IPv6 resources. Translation: they're not reselling someone else's IP space and praying the upstream doesn't pull the plug.

Their footprint covers Hong Kong (HGC and WTT data centers, plus Sha Tin Telecom), Los Angeles (600 West 7th Street and CoreSite LA2), Seoul (KT Mokdong and LG), and Tokyo (Equinix TY8). All facilities are billed as Tier III+ with 2N redundancy, and the company quotes a 99.982% availability figure — backed by a 99.9% uptime SLA with 100% compensation if they miss it.

The network story is the part most buyers care about: Hong Kong, US, Japan, and Korea nodes all carry direct connectivity to China Telecom's CN2 GIA, China Mobile's CMI, and China Unicom's backbone, plus BGP failover. For anyone running sites with a mainland China audience, that's the line that matters.

## What you're actually buying: the product lineup

HengHost isn't a single-product shop. The current portfolio breaks down into several distinct categories, and the right choice depends on what you're running.

**Cloud servers** are the headline product, available in Hong Kong, Japan, and the US. These are virtualized instances with configurable CPU, RAM, SSD storage, bandwidth, and traffic. Hong Kong offers four line types — BGP Premium, BGP Optimized, BGP International, and BGP High-Defense — while Japan and the US default to CN2 GIA and CN2+BGP respectively.

**Lightweight cloud servers** are a separate, simpler tier aimed at beginners and small sites. They ship with the Baota (aaPanel) control panel pre-installed and are sold on annual billing only, currently with a "buy 2 years, get 1 year free" bonus.

**Dedicated physical servers** give you the whole box — no noisy neighbors. Available in Hong Kong, Japan, and the US, with E3, E5, and Gold 6138 processor options. These come with a 24-hour free trial and free internal network setup between servers.

**Station group servers** pack 124 to 254 IPs across 1-8 C-segments on a single box, aimed at SEO operators running many sites.

**High-defense servers and high-defense IP services** handle DDoS mitigation, with Hong Kong defense up to 310 Gbps per server and US defense at Tbps+ level. Cloudflare advanced protection is integrated, with mitigation time claimed at under 3 seconds.

**Large bandwidth servers** start at 100 Mbps dedicated in Hong Kong (HGC/WTT) and go up to 300 Mbps on China-optimized lines.

**GPU servers** are the newest addition, currently featuring GeForce RTX 3090 (24GB GDDR6X) configurations aimed at AI training, 3D rendering, and machine learning workloads.

## How the network actually performs

The reason most people end up at HengHost is the CN2 GIA line. CN2 is China Telecom's next-generation backbone — less congested than the legacy 163 network, lower packet loss, more stable in evening peak hours. HengHost runs two flavors:

- **CN2 GIA**: the top-tier variant, end-to-end on Telecom's high-speed path. Best speed, slightly higher cost.
- **CN2 GT**: a hybrid where the international segment rides CN2 but the domestic segment can fall back to 163. Cheaper, but peak-hour congestion is possible.

Independent tests and community discussions consistently report Hong Kong CN2 GIA latency from mainland China in the 5-30ms range from southern cities, with Japan nodes sitting around 60-120ms and US nodes at 120-180ms. China Unicom traffic rides AS4837 optimized routes, and China Mobile uses CMI direct connectivity, so the experience doesn't fall off a cliff if your audience isn't on Telecom.

Disk I/O on the cloud instances typically benchmarks around 380+ MB/s, which is reasonable for the price tier — not NVMe-flagship territory, but solid for the kind of workloads these instances are built for.

## Things to know before you commit

A few details that aren't in the marketing copy but matter in practice:

**IP replacement policy**: 1-core 1GB and 1-core 2GB cloud plans don't include free IP swaps. Higher tiers get one free IP change, with charges from the second swap onward. If you need a clean IP for email reputation or specific geo, test it on delivery.

**Refund window**: 3-day no-questions refund on promotional products. After 3 days, you're committed. Run your speed tests, latency checks, and stability benchmarks immediately on provisioning.

**Billing cycle lock-in for renewal pricing**: the renewal-price-match guarantee only holds if you keep the same billing cycle. Switch from annual to monthly renewal and you revert to list price. Plan your commitment accordingly.

**Bandwidth upgrades don't carry promo pricing**: if you buy a discounted plan and later want more bandwidth or RAM, the upgrade is billed at standard rates, not the promo rate. Size your initial purchase with headroom.

**Native IP availability**: default IPs on Japan cloud servers are non-native. Native Japanese IPs are limited supply and require a support ticket. For Hong Kong, default IPs are standard BGP; native Hong Kong IPs cost an additional ¥200/month/IP.

**Windows support**: requires 2-core 2GB minimum. Don't try to squeeze Windows onto a 1-core instance — it'll boot, but you'll regret it.

**Documentation language**: English documentation exists but is thinner than the Chinese version. Support is available in both languages plus Telegram, but complex custom configurations may need a bit of back-and-forth.
